Infrastructure & networks

01 Network Infrastructure & OT/SCADA Security Switching, VLANs and firewalls across HQ and remote sites, with SCADA segments kept apart from corporate IT.
Context
A multi-site network — HQ plus remote sites — with operational-technology (SCADA) segments running alongside corporate IT.
Problem
Mixed IT/OT environments need consistent connectivity and clear segmentation, so a corporate-side issue can never reach control systems.
My role
Maintain HPE and Aruba switches, access points, IP phones and firewalls across sites, and support the vulnerability-assessment and risk-analysis work on the OT side.
Approach
Run preventive maintenance on the switching, trace and document patch-panel ports, and keep the VLAN and equipment workbook accurate. Configure Palo Alto (PAN-OS) rules with service and FQDN objects, deliberate policy placement and SSL-decryption exclusions, then verify them in the logs. On the OT side, drafted a guideline for controlling automatic Windows Updates on plant PCs so an unplanned update can't disrupt control-system software.
Outcome
Stable multi-site connectivity, with assessment findings prioritised into a concrete list of gaps and mitigations. The cleaned-up workbook also became the specification for seven inventory dashboards, from port utilisation to asset lifecycle.
Lessons
Segmentation only works when it is enforced everywhere — least privilege between IT and OT segments is what keeps corporate incidents away from control systems.
  • HPE / Aruba
  • VLANs
  • Palo Alto PAN-OS
  • SCADA / OT
  • Network Documentation
02 Proxmox Virtualisation & Backup Management Proxmox VE nodes running Active Directory and business workloads, with scheduled backups, snapshots and replication.
Context
Production virtualisation for Active Directory and business workloads across multiple Proxmox nodes.
Problem
Workloads needed reliable provisioning, isolation, and a tested recovery path — not just backups that are assumed to work.
My role
Provisioned and maintained the VMs and LXC containers, designed the backup and replication schedule, and owned disaster-recovery readiness.
Approach
Configured storage pools and resource monitoring, isolated production with firewall rules, scheduled PBS backups and snapshots, and replicated critical workloads between nodes.
Outcome
Reliable virtualisation across nodes, with scheduled PBS backups, snapshots, and replication in place for disaster-recovery readiness.
Lessons
An untested backup is not a backup — restore checks matter as much as the schedule, and replication frequency is a direct trade-off against how much data you can afford to lose.
  • Proxmox
  • Virtualisation
  • PBS
  • Backup
  • Linux
  • Active Directory
03 Company-Wide Remote Access Deployment Splashtop rolled out to around 70 endpoints, so routine support no longer needs a site visit.
Context
A renewable-energy company with an HQ and multiple remote sites, where routine IT support still meant travelling to the affected site.
Problem
No standardised remote-support tooling — troubleshooting meant travel and delays across departments.
My role
Owned the rollout end-to-end — tool selection, access-policy design, endpoint enrolment, and staff enablement.
Approach
Defined role-based access policies, enrolled endpoints in waves to avoid disruption, enforced MFA and session logging, and ran short per-department training sessions.
Outcome
Around 70 endpoints enrolled in Splashtop remote access — routine issues are now resolved remotely instead of requiring on-site visits.
Lessons
Phased enrolment kept support available throughout the rollout, and mapping access policies to roles up front prevented over-permissioning later.
  • Splashtop

Security & compliance

04 Windows 11 Hardening Audit A 137-control PowerShell audit that checks a workstation against CIS Benchmarks and ISO/IEC 27001:2022.
Context
A host-hardening assessment of a Windows 11 Pro workstation against an internal checklist mapped to CIS Benchmarks and ISO/IEC 27001:2022.
Problem
Checking well over a hundred controls by hand is slow, hard to repeat and easy to get wrong.
My role
Wrote the audit script, ran the assessment and delivered the findings.
Approach
A PowerShell script evaluates each of the 137 controls and writes Pass, Fail or Manual to CSV. The results fed an HTML findings report and the completed PDF and Word checklists.
Outcome
A repeatable audit: one script run plus a short list of manual checks in place of a long hand-worked checklist. It also showed that devices joined only to Entra ID sit outside Group Policy, so lasting enforcement has to go through Intune.
Lessons
Re-test after every fix. One registry change caused a regression in HVCI and Credential Guard that then had to be diagnosed.
  • PowerShell
  • CIS Benchmarks
  • ISO/IEC 27001:2022
  • Windows 11
  • Intune
05 Endpoint Compliance with Intune & Defender USB storage whitelisting, a screen-lock policy pilot and a roughly 30-user rollout test for managed Windows devices.
Context
Endpoint controls for ISO/IEC 27001 alignment on a Microsoft 365 estate managed with Intune and Defender for Endpoint.
Problem
Removable media and unattended screens needed enforced, auditable controls, and the managed-device setup had to be proven with real users before a wider rollout.
My role
Deployed the policies alongside the implementation vendor and ran user acceptance testing.
Approach
Rolled a screensaver policy out to a pilot group first, whitelisted approved USB storage through Group Policy and Intune to meet the removable-media control, and ran UAT for Intune and Defender for Endpoint with about 30 users.
Outcome
Pilot policies in place and UAT run with about 30 users, with each policy tied to a named ISO 27001 control.
Lessons
A pilot group surfaces the exceptions — the devices and people who need a different rule — before a policy reaches everyone.
  • Intune
  • Defender for Endpoint
  • Group Policy
  • ISO/IEC 27001
  • UAT
06 Security Incident Response & Staff Awareness Handled a social-engineering malware attempt and a scareware incident, then turned both into staff awareness.
Context
Two user-facing security events: a suspicious file sent over a messaging app by someone posing as a contractor, and fake antivirus pop-ups delivered through browser notifications.
Problem
Both depend on a person clicking. Fixing one device isn't enough if the rest of the staff can't recognise the next attempt.
My role
Led the response to the social-engineering attempt and remediated the scareware incident.
Approach
For the malware attempt: confirmed it was contained, classified it as a medium-severity near miss and wrote a formal incident report aligned to ISO/IEC 27001:2022. For the scareware: blocked the malicious domain in the browser, verified Defender and ran a full scan. Each was followed by a plain-language awareness message to staff, including an infographic.
Outcome
The malware attempt was contained as a near miss, the scareware was removed, and staff received clear guidance on spotting both.
Lessons
A near miss is the cheapest training material there is. Awareness sent while the event is fresh lands better than a policy reminder.
  • Incident Response
  • ISO/IEC 27001:2022
  • Microsoft Defender
  • Security Awareness

Microsoft 365 & automation

07 PowerShell Automation Suite for IT Operations Onboarding, server cleanup and disk monitoring in one PowerShell suite, with rollback built in.
Context
Recurring IT operations work: creating accounts for new joiners, clearing temp and log files on Windows Servers, and watching disk space.
Problem
Repeated manual steps take time and invite mistakes, and automated cleanup on a server is risky without a way back.
My role
Designed and built the suite.
Approach
Three tools on a shared core module: new-user onboarding across Active Directory and Microsoft 365 through the Graph API, with a welcome email; scheduled temp and log cleanup that quarantines files before removing them; and disk-space monitoring that sends HTML email reports. Credentials are stored with DPAPI and SecretManagement, a rollback engine reverses changes, and a README documents the restore procedures.
Outcome
One documented, reversible toolkit covering onboarding, cleanup and monitoring.
Lessons
Build the undo first. Quarantine and rollback are what make it safe to schedule a script against a production server.
  • PowerShell
  • Microsoft Graph
  • Active Directory
  • Microsoft 365
  • SecretManagement
08 Microsoft 365 & SharePoint Administration Identity, licensing and least-privilege access for the whole organisation, with the repetitive parts scripted.
Context
Day-to-day identity and collaboration administration for the whole organisation on Microsoft 365 and Entra ID.
Problem
Access, licensing, and permissions had to stay correct and least-privilege as staff joined, moved between roles, and left.
My role
Owned user provisioning, group and RBAC design, licensing, and SharePoint permissions.
Approach
Standardised joiner/mover/leaver handling, enforced role-based permissions and group policies, and reviewed licensing regularly for cost and compliance. Pulled and de-duplicated department data from the tenant with Microsoft Graph PowerShell, and built a Power Automate flow that sends weekly new-joiner reminders.
Outcome
Consistent, least-privilege access across the organisation, with secure workspaces and licensing kept aligned to actual need.
In progress
A SharePoint storage-governance proposal covering offboarding, versioning, archiving and site creation — housekeeping first, before buying more storage.
Lessons
Group-based access and licensing scale far better than per-user changes, and scripting the repetitive parts removes both toil and human error.

  • Microsoft Graph
  • Power Automate
09 IT Help Center on SharePoint One self-service hub for guides, FAQs, IT requests and announcements, used by all employees.
Context
Employees had no central place for IT guides, and the same questions kept reaching the IT team through scattered channels.
Problem
Repetitive support load and no single source of truth for how-to guides, FAQs, or requests.
My role
Designed, built, and maintain the SharePoint Help Center end-to-end — structure, content, and the request workflow.
Approach
Organised a knowledge base of guides and FAQs by category, added an IT request form and an announcements area, and laid out the site so staff can self-serve before raising a ticket.
Outcome
Fewer repeat questions reaching IT, and one self-service hub every employee knows to check first.

Information architecture drives adoption — staff only self-serve when the answer is easier to find than it is to ask.

10 Endpoint & Device Lifecycle Provisioning, onboarding and asset tracking for the device fleet, plus a portable diagnostic tool for first-pass troubleshooting.
Context
End-to-end device lifecycle and IT onboarding for staff across the organisation.
Problem
Inconsistent onboarding and ad-hoc asset tracking risked setup delays, downtime, and unaccounted-for equipment.
My role
Owned provisioning, onboarding, asset inventory, and hardware/software troubleshooting.
Approach
Standardised the onboarding checklist and device build, kept a single asset inventory as the source of truth, and triaged issues to minimise downtime.
Tooling
Built PC Health & Crash Analyzer, a Python/tkinter tool compiled to a single .exe with PyInstaller so it runs from USB. It reads hardware specs and Event Logs, detects blue screens and hanging apps, checks Windows Update and BitLocker status, and exports the result to CSV or PDF.
Outcome
Faster, more consistent onboarding, with every device accounted for from provisioning to retirement.
In progress
A digital laptop surrender-and-undertaking form with Microsoft 365 sign-in, a SharePoint list for IT verification and a serial-number check against the asset inventory.
Lessons
A single source of truth for assets pays for itself — most slow-setup and "missing device" problems trace back to tracking, not hardware.
  • Endpoint Management
  • IT Support
  • Device Provisioning
  • Asset Inventory
  • Python
